This module extends the US Sales Tax Engine
(``l10n_us_sales_tax_engine``) for companies organised in branches, and
lets sales be rated with the address of the company or branch that sells
instead of the customer's address.

- **Nexus inherited from parent companies.** A branch collects tax in a
  state when it, or any of its parent companies at any depth, has an
  active nexus there. A nexus declared on a sibling branch does not
  count.
- **Origin-based sourcing.** Each company chooses whether new sales
  orders and customer invoices are rated, by default, with the
  customer's delivery address (destination) or with the company address
  (origin). Each draft document keeps its own *Tax Based on Company
  Address* check, so a branch can mix in-store pickups and shipped
  orders. This covers origin-based states and in-store sales in
  destination-based states.

Every calculation log records whether the customer's or the company's
address was rated.

Use Cases / Context
===================

The US Sales Tax Engine (``l10n_us_sales_tax_engine``) is designed for a
single company that ships to its customers: it looks for the nexus on
the exact company of the document and always rates the tax with an
address of the customer. Companies organised in branches, and companies
that collect tax at the point of sale, need more than that:

- A branch should collect tax wherever its parent company has nexus.
  With the engine alone, a nexus declared only on the parent company
  leaves the branches exempt, so the same nexus has to be repeated on
  every branch.
- In origin-based states the rate is the one of the seller's location,
  and in destination-based states a sale picked up in the store is taxed
  where the store is. Both cases need the address of the selling company
  or branch instead of the customer's.

This module keeps the engine's calculation and only changes which nexus
and which address it uses. It does not hold a list of origin-based
states: each company chooses its default sourcing and each document can
override it. The origin address is the company address, not the one of a
warehouse.

It is useful in multi-company setups that use branches (companies with a
parent company), and also for a single company that sells over the
counter. A company without a parent that keeps destination sourcing is
rated exactly as with the engine alone.

Configuration
=============

To configure this module, you need to:

1. Select, in the company switcher, the company or branch to configure:
   the setting is stored per company.
2. Go to *Settings > Invoicing > US Sales Tax Engine*. The *Tax
   Sourcing* setting is only shown when *Enable US Sales Tax Engine* is
   checked.
3. Choose the *Tax Sourcing* of the company:

   - *Destination (customer address)*, the default: new documents are
     rated with the customer's delivery address, as with the engine
     alone.
   - *Origin (company address)*: new sales orders and customer invoices
     are rated with the company address. The company needs a 5-digit
     ZIP, a state and United States as country; otherwise the setting
     cannot be saved.

4. Save.

|Tax Sourcing setting|

Changing the setting only affects documents created afterwards. Existing
documents keep the check they already have; the ones that exist when the
module is installed start with the check off, so their rating does not
change.

To declare the nexus of a company tree:

1. Go to *Invoicing > Configuration > US Sales Tax > Configuration >
   Nexus*.
2. Create the nexus on the parent company. It applies to the parent and
   to all its branches, so there is no need to repeat it on each branch.
3. A branch cannot cancel the nexus of a parent: while the parent's
   nexus is active, the branch collects tax in that state even if its
   own nexus record is archived. A nexus on a sibling branch does not
   apply.

|Nexus declared on the parent company|

With origin sourcing, the nexus that matters is the one in the state of
the company address, since that is the address being rated.

Usage
=====

To use this module, you need to:

1. Create a sales order or a customer invoice. The *Tax Based on Company
   Address* check comes:

   - checked when the company uses *Origin* sourcing, unless the
     customer's delivery country is set and is not the United States;
   - unchecked when the company uses *Destination* sourcing.

2. Leave the check on to rate the tax with the address of the document's
   company or branch, for example for an in-store pickup. Uncheck it to
   rate with the customer's address: on the sales order, *Tax Based on
   Shipping Address* shows again and chooses between the customer's
   delivery and invoice addresses; customer invoices always use the
   delivery address.
3. Click *Calculate US Tax*, or confirm the order. With the check on,
   the rate is the one of the company ZIP and the tax is named after the
   company's state (``US Sales Tax <state> <rate>%``).

The check can be changed while the order is a quotation (sent or not)
and while the invoice is in draft. It is recomputed when the company or
the delivery address of the document changes. Changing the company
setting, or the state or country of a customer, does not change existing
documents.

When the check is on and the delivery address is in a different state
from the company's, the draft order or invoice shows a warning. It does
not block anything: uncheck *Tax Based on Company Address* to rate with
the delivery address instead.

|Quotation rated with the company address|

Invoices:

- An invoice created from a sales order takes the order's check. Orders
  with a different value are never grouped in the same invoice.
- A credit note keeps the check of its invoice.
- Posting a customer invoice rates it again with its own check.

|Draft customer invoice rated with the company address|

Special cases:

- Customer outside the United States: no US tax, as with the engine
  alone, even if the check is on.
- Customer without an address (counter sale): rated with the company
  address when the check is on.
- Company without a complete US address (5-digit ZIP, state and United
  States as country) on a document with the check on: the customer's
  address is used and a warning is written to the server log.

In *Invoicing > Configuration > US Sales Tax > Audit > Calculation
Logs*, the *Tax Sourcing* column, also shown in the *Address* group of
each log, tells whether the rated address was the customer's
(*Destination*) or the company's (*Origin*). The address fields of the
log show the address actually rated.
